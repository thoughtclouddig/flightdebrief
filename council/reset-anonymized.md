# AFTERFLIGHT RESET COUNCIL — SIX ANONYMIZED ADVISOR RESPONSES

QUESTION: Should AfterFlight stop being the school's debrief/training-management system and become the PILOT'S AI TRAINING COACH -- student pays ~$19.99/mo, CFI free and distributes, school optional? Or continue school-facing, become an intelligence layer, materially change, or STOP?

Evidence base: council/reset-evidence.md (live research 31 Aug 2026).

---
## RESPONSE A

**1. Is there an ownable category? Yes -- but it is not "the pilot's AI training coach."** That phrase is a feature bundle in a costume. PilotTrack already owns almost the exact words at $29.99. "Coach" is a role, not a category, and every competitor can claim it by Tuesday.

The ownable position is narrower and harder to copy: **the readiness verdict from how you actually flew.** Student words: *"It tells me if I'm actually ready to solo -- based on my real flights, not a quiz."*

That is ownable because the claim requires something nobody else assembles: the student's real flight performance (which only FlightSense, Atlas, Navi hold, and none reason over) plus pedagogy (which only quiz-shops do, on the wrong data). PilotTrack's "86% ready" is a quiz score wearing a flight-hours costume -- and if you say it out loud in your copy, they can't answer it without rebuilding their input data.

**2. The seam is an observation, not yet a category.** "Nobody crosses" is a supply-side fact. Categories are demand-side. The counter-evidence: MasterPilot, Aviate AI, Redbird Pro -- AI post-flight feedback, priced right, backed by real companies -- 3, 11, and 6 ratings. Meanwhile radio phraseology, a *specific feared testable skill*, has 325 and 244. The seam is unfilled partly because the buyer does not feel post-flight synthesis as a problem. What makes readiness different from generic feedback is that readiness is *feared, dated, and attributable* -- the solo and the checkride are events with a date and a person who says yes or no.

What would settle it: 40 students shown two landing pages at $19.99 -- "your AI training coach" vs "know if you're ready to solo, from your real flights" -- and measure card-entry, not clicks. Second test: buy the Ahrefs seat and get volume on "am I ready to solo/checkride." If nobody searches it, the category is imaginary.

**3. Strongest for:** an explainable readiness verdict grounded in real flight performance exists nowhere, and FlightSense's own "track checkride readiness" is a pricing bullet with no shipped feature. They named the demand and didn't build it.
**Strongest against:** Redbird Pro is $15.99-19.99/mo, backed by GA's biggest sim maker, five years in market, six ratings. That is your exact price, exact buyer, better distribution, and it failed. No positioning fixes six ratings.

**4. The FlightSense test.** *"FlightSense shows my instructor's grades. AfterFlight tells me whether I'm ready to solo yet and what's blocking it."* That sentence is **medium, not strong.** It is weak in the state that matters most: a school-seated student pays $0, and their CFI already answers "are you ready to solo" for free, in person, with authority you cannot claim. Your verdict competes with a human whose signature is legally required. Where it is genuinely strong is the guest-with-no-subscription state and the independent Part 61 student with no school platform at all. That is your beachhead, and it is smaller than the brief assumes.

**5. Stop saying:** "The pilot's AI training coach" (taken, generic). "Student-owned portable record" and "the handoff" (FlightSense ships both, leads with handoff). "Turns each debrief into personalized instruction" (that is Aviate AI's pitch -- eleven ratings). "CFI talks normally, system infers structure" (a build detail, not a reason to pay). Any percentage-style readiness number (PilotTrack owns "86% ready"). "$19.99 for students" as settled (2x the education median with 24% annual renewal).

## RESPONSE B

**1. The cohort model -- 1,000 paying subs at $19.99.** Retention assumption: front-loaded decay, M1->M2 at 58%, easing to ~90%/mo by M10. Twelve-month survival ~9.3%. Defense: RevenueCat's AI benchmark is 6.1% monthly-plan survival at 12 months; non-AI is 9.5%. I sit AfterFlight *between* them -- better than the AI median because student pilots are high-intent inside a funded goal, worse than non-AI because (a) 2-4 uses/month means no habit anchor, (b) ~80% of students quit with the cliff at hours 10-20 = months 1.5-3, exactly where my curve is steepest, (c) the AI novelty spike is real (52% better trial conversion) and it collapses.

M1 1,000 subs $19,990 · M2 580 · M3 400 · M4 304 · M5 243 · M6 202 · M7 172 · M8 148 · M9 130 · M10 115 · M11 103 · M12 93. Cumulative $69,766.

3,490 subscriber-months / 1,000 = **3.49 collected months. Realistic LTV = $69.77 gross; $66.70 net on web (Stripe); $48.84 net through Apple year-1.** Not $119.94, not $239.88. It is 3x the education-category median ($22.82), which is the honest good news.

**2. Price and structure.** Kill monthly as the default. Education's 58% weekly renewal says the same thing as checkride.bot's $49.99/30-days-no-auto-renew: **this category pays for a goal, not a service.** Recommendation: **$149 one-time, "Through Your Checkride" -- 6 months, no auto-renew, renewable at $99.** Offer month-to-month only at **$29/mo** as a deliberately worse option. $149 collects 2.1x the $69.77 a monthly cohort actually yields; it survives even if it converts 50% worse. Cash lands day one, refund exposure closes at 30 days, and there is no month-2 cancel decision -- the highest-mortality moment -- at all.

**3. Thresholds (web billing, $66.70/sub).** Validation: 100 paying + month-3 retention >=45%. Solo-founder sustainable ($150k net): ~654 concurrent, $13.1k MRR, ~2% of the 59k annual new-entrant TAM -- **achievable**. Attractive small SaaS ($1M ARR): ~14,400 new subs/yr = **24% of every new US student pilot annually.** Venture scale ($10M): 240% of the entire annual cohort. **Impossible in this ICP.**

**4. The $25 referral is not survivable.** $25 = **51% of Apple year-1 net LTV** and 37% of web net -- before any other CAC, on a subscriber whose median life is 3.49 months. The $50 founding rate is 102% of Apple net. Both negative-margin. Replace with the ForeFlight design: CFI free forever + student-roster dashboard + subscription credit, not cash. King Schools runs GA's largest CFI program on $0 cash. If cash must exist: $25 *only* on a $149 term purchase (16.8%), capped 5/CFI/quarter.

**5. The deciding metric: month-3 paid retention.** Stop threshold: **below 40% at month 3** -- under 2.9 collected months. At that point web LTV is under $58, Apple LTV under $41, no paid channel closes, and the referral cannot be funded from revenue. Measure it in the pilot BEFORE building the quiz engine or the micro-lesson generator.

## RESPONSE C

**1. Product core or feature bundle? Feature bundle -- with exactly one real core inside it.** "Debrief -> Teach -> Quiz -> Next Flight" is four surfaces of which three are commodity or unproven. The *Teach* step is weakest: nobody has shown students want post-flight micro-lessons, generated prose is the most copyable artifact in the stack, and AI-personalization apps retain *worse* (21.1% vs 30.7%). MasterPilot/Aviate/Redbird already sell "AI tells you about your flight" and have 3, 11, 6 ratings.

The core is the **Quiz** -- reframed. Not a quiz: **graded voice recall of the student's own last flight.** The hole is real (zero retrieval practice anywhere in FlightSense; the student never answers a question). The seam is real (nobody joins real flight data to real pedagogy). The pricing power is real (voice recitation of maneuvers/procedures does not exist; only *radio* voice grading exists and it bottoms out at free). Teach becomes the *response to a wrong answer*, not the product. That ordering is also correct learning design -- reflection before feedback -- and it makes the lesson cheap instead of load-bearing.

**2. Smallest test: the Flight-Grounded Voice Check.** Trigger 18-24h after a debrief. 3 questions generated from that debrief's needs-work items and next-lesson focus. Student answers **by voice**. Graded, scored, and **written back to training_signals** so it feeds recurrence and skill trends. Instrument open rate, completion rate, repeat rate at weeks 2, 4, 8. Reuse Deepgram STT, Claude, the radio module's AI judge and scoring harness, ACS mapping, recurrence, TTS. ~two weeks, not a rebuild.
**Do NOT build yet:** the generated micro-lesson (ship 3 lines of "why you missed that" on a wrong answer only); spaced-repetition scheduler; checkride readiness verdict; landscape workspace; the 12-screen prototype; any new UI shell. If students won't answer three questions about their own flight, none of the rest matters.

**3. Force-rank** (Pain/Urg/WTP/Acq/CFI-dep(low better)/Sch-dep/Cplx/Diff/Reten/Fit/Def):
- Chair Flying (maneuver recitation) 7/6/5/9/1/1/6/9/9/8/5 -- **BUILD NOW**
- Checkride Readiness 9/10/9/8/4/2/5/8/3/8/7 -- **BUILD NOW (as the paywall)**
- Solo Readiness 8/7/3/3/6/2/3/6/8/9/6 -- **BUILD NOW (cheap)**
- Radio Coach (built) 8/7/2/9/1/1/1/2/7/6/2 -- **PROTOTYPE -> freeze**
- Lesson Prep / Next Flight 5/4/3/2/7/2/2/4/6/9/4 -- **LATER (stays inside core)**
- Meta / multimodal 3/1/2/2/1/1/10/10/4/5/9 -- **DO NOT BUILD (sim PoC only, timeboxed)**

Justifying the awkward ones: **Chair flying** scores 5 on WTP/defensibility, and that reconciles the free-race objection -- graded voice races to free *for radio* because radio phraseology is a fixed, public, finite corpus; anyone can build it and four vendors did. Maneuver recitation graded **against your instructor's stated emphasis from your last debrief** is not a corpus problem; the input is proprietary. Build it only in that bound form -- never as a standalone maneuver library. **Checkride Readiness** gets retention 3: it is terminal, the student passes and cancels; price it as a $59-79 one-time SKU, not MRR. **Solo Readiness** gets WTP 3 and still BUILD NOW: nearly free to build and it lands exactly on the 10-20 hour dropout cliff -- a retention asset, not a revenue asset. **Radio Coach** differentiation 2, WTP 2: the only category with demonstrated pull (244-325 ratings) *and* worth ~$0. Wire it into training_signals so it stops being an island, then **freeze it** as free top-of-funnel. **Meta** defensibility 9, complexity 10 -- the only genuine moat and the wrong decade.

**4. The retention mechanic -- there is one, and it is not a streak.** Education apps retain best "when offered plans that match the duration of their goal," and education has the highest weekly renewal (58%). The mechanic: **a dated, finite rehearsal plan pinned to the student's next booked lesson.** Not "content available between flights" -- "3 voice sessions before Tuesday, and here's what Jake will ask you." The reason to open the app is not habit; it's a deadline with another human on the other end. Flying already supplies the cadence -- attach to it. Two consequences: (a) capture the next flight date at debrief close -- the single most important field you don't collect; (b) $19.99/mo is wrong -- price the milestone: "Solo Prep, 8 weeks, $49" / "Checkride Prep, 6 weeks, $79." **If the voice check's week-8 repeat rate is under ~25%, there is no mechanic and the honest answer is STOP.**

**5. Remove:** school/admin views + multi-org (if school isn't the payer, they earn nothing and anchor you to a conceded fight); FlightScore (a single number contradicts "why you're not ready" and reads as an instructor scorecard); study references + open-tracking (replace with the voice check); the handoff brief *as a lead wedge* (keep the feature, kill the positioning); the "student-owned portable record" claim; Debrief Replay TTS as a headline; the 12-screen prototype and landscape workspace; the 3-free-flights entitlement (wrong unit -- free should be time-boxed to a milestone).

## RESPONSE D

**1. Is the CFI channel real? No -- not as the primary acquisition engine.** 12,000 reachable unique engaged CFIs x 3% outreach-to-signup = 360 enrolled x 30% active in a quarter = ~108 active x 4 students pitched x 25% trial->paid = **~108 paying subscribers/year**, ~$13k gross. Against $15-25k for a single postal touch of the FAA database. **Negative-margin at realistic volumes.** Best case (8% enroll, 50% active, 6 students, 30% conv) = 864 subs -- the ceiling, not the plan, and it requires beating ForeFlight's activation cold.

**2. The CFI offer: no cash. Free tool plus dashboard.** Five vendors converged because cash fails arithmetic the CFI does instantly: $375/yr is a rounding error against $50-80/hr. ForeFlight's Dec 2025 swap of a discount for a referral program produced anger -- CFIs read referral cash as a *downgrade*. Give: free forever, unlimited, no student cap, never a paid CFI tier; **the Instructor Continuity Board** (every student, last debrief, unresolved items, what they got wrong on the knowledge check, next-lesson focus, pre-populated) -- this returns unpaid prep time, the only currency that moves a CFI; the synthesized handoff brief; zero-install lightweight mode. If any incentive stays: **account credit, never cash**, and require CFI disclosure to employer in the terms.

**3. Channel ranking by est. CAC:** organic/SEO on checkride+solo intent $15-40 · free Chair Flying surface $20-50 · AOPA FTE list (4,200 pre-qualified) $60-120 · SAFE partner slot $80-150 · NAFI partner-discount slot $100-200 · paid social $150-300 · FAA postal $300-600+ (don't).
**Test first: the free Chair Flying / maneuver-recitation surface, direct to student.** This week: ship one graded voice scenario ("talk me through a power-off 180 from memory") as a no-login public page, wire the existing radio-practice scoring to it, buy the $150 Semrush seat to size "chair flying," "am I ready for my checkride," "power off 180 steps."

**4. Direct-to-student alternative:** checkride-readiness search intent (highest urgency and WTP; check-ride.ai proves it converts); the 15-hour cliff ("why am I not soloing yet"); YouTube CFI creators (Pilot Institute gates its 20% to creators *because that's where volume is*); r/flying via genuine participation. Buy the keyword seat before writing another word of content.

**5. The 20 Founding CFI test is structured to succeed cosmetically.** 20 x 5 = 100 students is a number chosen for the slide, not from the base rate; $50/student means you pay for referrals you'd have gotten anyway, then declare the channel proven. Fix: **cut the cash to $0** (if 20 CFIs won't take a free unlimited tool plus dashboard, cash won't save it -- and you learn the real answer for free); recruit **40, not 20**; **90-day hard stop**. Pass: >=50% of enrolled CFIs refer >=1 student; >=3 referred students per active CFI; >=30% trial->paid; >=60% of paying students active at day 60. Fail: <30% refer anyone, OR <2 students per active CFI, OR day-60 retention <40%. **Instrument the dormancy rate** -- how many enroll and never refer. That's the number no vendor publishes and it's your actual answer. Run the direct-to-student Chair Flying test **in parallel**, not after.

## RESPONSE E

**1. The $20/month question.** Honestly? My first reaction is no. I'm already out $260 for ForeFlight, $299 for Sporty's, and roughly $12,000 in Hobbs time. Another $240/yr is a lesson and a half of dual. When I'm deciding between "subscribe" and "fly one more hour this month," the hour wins every time.

The moment I'd pay: I've just had a lesson that went badly, I don't know why, my CFI said something about "you're chasing the airplane in the flare" that I nodded at and did not understand, and I open my phone and something explains it to me in a way that makes it click. If that happens once, I'll pay. **That's the trigger. Not a feature list -- one moment of "oh, THAT'S what he meant."**

The moment I'd cancel: the second it tells me something generic. If I get a lesson that reads like a Wikipedia paragraph with my name pasted in, I know instantly it didn't listen to my flight, and I'm done. Also: if I don't fly for three weeks because of weather and money -- which happens constantly -- and I get billed anyway for an app I opened zero times, I cancel that month. **You should probably let me pause.**

**2. The micro-lesson.** Not right after the lesson -- I'm cooked, it's 8pm, I have a standup at 9am. What I actually do post-flight is drive home and eat. Where I WOULD open it: next morning at my desk, or two days later at lunch. Or -- the real one -- **the night before my next flight**, when I'm nervous and want to not repeat what I did wrong. How many before I stop: three or four. If lessons 1-3 tell me something my CFI didn't already say out loud, I keep going. If they're restatements, I quit by #3 and never come back. What makes me open it that a notification wouldn't: **hearing it. If it's audio and I can play it in the car on the drive home, I'll listen every single time, because I'm already replaying the flight in my head anyway. Reading is work. Listening while driving is free.**

**3. The quiz.** If it's "What is the standard rate turn?" -- annoying, patronizing, delete. I just paid $180 to be evaluated for an hour. If it's **"You were high and fast on the third landing. What's the first thing you fix?"** -- that's not a quiz, that's my CFI asking me a question, and I'd answer it. Two or three questions, about MY flight, and **never show me a score.** Show me a score and it becomes another thing I'm failing at.

**4. The perception gap.** It stings, and I think that's fine. First feeling is embarrassment, not motivation. Then ten minutes later it turns into "okay, what is he seeing that I'm not?" -- a useful question I'd never have asked. But HOW you say it matters enormously. "Your instructor rated you lower" makes me feel judged. **"Jake wants more from your crosswind landings than you think you're giving -- here's specifically what" makes me want to fix it. Don't ever show me a gap without immediately showing me the fix in the same breath.** Would I want Jake to see my self-rating? Yes -- but only my rating, not "the gap." It should read as me telling him what I'm confused about, not the app catching me being overconfident.

**5. What I actually want that nobody sells me.** Two things, I'd pay for both today without thinking. First: **am I behind?** I'm at 28 hours with a 35-hour minimum I'm obviously not going to hit, I've had two instructors, and nobody has ever told me whether that's normal or whether I'm the slow one. **I lie awake about this.** I've spent $12,000 and I genuinely do not know if I'm 60% done or 40% done. Tell me honestly where I am against people like me -- same hours-per-week, same aircraft, same weather -- and what specifically is costing me hours. Second: **what did he actually say?** I forget half of every debrief within a day. Between two instructors I've had things explained twice, differently, and I couldn't tell you which one I'm supposed to be doing. A searchable, plain-language record of everything my instructors have told me would be worth $20 by itself. Also, quietly: what a lesson is likely to cost me and whether I'm burning money on a maneuver I should have nailed six flights ago. **Money anxiety is the actual background hum of this whole thing, and nobody talks to me about it.**

**6. Chair flying and checkride readiness.** Chair flying, **genuinely yes** -- I already do it badly on my couch moving my hand around, and I have no idea if I'm doing it right. "Talk me through a short-field takeoff at our home field, go" and then telling me I forgot flaps -- I'd use that on a weeknight when I can't fly. **That's the thing that keeps me engaged during the two weeks it rains. I'd pay for that on its own.** Checkride readiness -- I'm 28 hours in, not my problem yet. Ask me at 55 and I'd throw money at it. But right now **solo readiness** is what I care about and nobody has told me anything. My CFI has hinted twice and not committed. **If your app told me "here are the four things standing between you and solo, and here's number one" -- I would pay $20 a month for that alone, this month, immediately.**

The rest -- progress dashboards, explainable readiness percentages, the training workspace on a tablet -- sounds like something a founder is excited about. **I have never once wished for a landscape training workspace. I've wished I knew whether I was going to solo before I ran out of money.**

## RESPONSE F

**1. The product exists. Nobody buys it.** MasterPilot 3 ratings/2.3 stars. Aviate AI 11. Redbird Pro -- backed by the largest sim manufacturer in GA, five years -- 6. CloudAhoy, fourteen years -- 35. Radio phraseology: 244 and 325. Same buyers, same wallets, same price band. A ~50x gap. This is not distribution, messaging, or execution. Four independent teams, one with the deepest channel in GA, all landed in single digits. Pilots pay for a specific, feared, testable skill. They do not pay for AI-generated reflection about a flight they already flew. **AfterFlight is proposing to become the fifth entry in the only aviation category with a documented zero.**

**2. The thesis is moving faster than the evidence.** On 30 August: perception gap is the wedge, sell to schools. On 31 August: students pay, AI Trainer is the core. Twenty-four hours. Nothing in the market changed -- only what was discovered about one competitor's website. **The pivot is not the finding. The pivot rate is the finding.**

**3. Unit economics dead on arrival.** AI apps 21.1% annual / 6.1% monthly. Education 24% annual, second worst. Median education Year-1 realized LTV: **$22.82**. The proposed $25 referral exceeds the median lifetime value of the customer it buys -- before Stripe, before Apple's 30%, before support. And the customer leaves anyway: 80% quit, clustered at hours 10-20, inside the first three billing cycles. 2-4 events/month means no habit loop. $19.99 is 2x the education median. **The founder named churn as the top risk and then modeled 6-, 8-, and 12-month LTVs anyway. The honest number is under three.**

**4. FlightSense already took the student.** $15/month, marketed verbatim "For student pilots." Portable records on an 8-character ID. Handoff as the lead wedge. A school-seated student gets unlimited Amelia at $0. AfterFlight must beat $15 *and* free-at-point-of-use, with a weaker data position, from zero installed base.

**5. The differentiators are one sprint wide.** The perception gap needs a student-side rating field and a join -- FlightSense already has student self-debrief, they just made it read-only. A product decision, reversible in a week. Graded voice races to free. **Chair flying is unsold not because it is white space but because an unmonetized gap is usually a grave, not a claim.** TTS is commodity and the brief admits it.

**6. The channel is six times smaller than assumed.** 23,649 CFIs signed off an applicant in 2025 -- and it *fell*. No vendor publishes referral results anywhere. **Silence from marketers is not a gap in the research. Silence is the result.**

**7. The research keeps trailing reality.** He thought debrief-quality scoring was open -- FlightSense ships it. Handoff open -- FlightSense leads with it. Portable records open -- FlightSense ships them. Three for three, all discovered *after* the strategy was written on top of them. There is no reason to believe the fourth survives the next look. It never has yet.

**8. The glasses are the tell.** Multimodal smart-glasses capture appears in a brief about a product that cannot retain a $20 subscriber for three months. Founders reach for a ceiling-changing vision precisely when the floor gives way. It is not ambition. It is escape.

**And the sunk cost.** Everything is built: perception gap, recurrence, handoff brief, replay, radio module, billing. That is exactly the problem. He is not asking "should this exist" -- he is asking "what is this for," which is the question you only ask about something you already own. The radio module -- adjacent to the *only* category in this market that actually sells -- was built and left as an island that does not even write to training_signals. **The built asset is not evidence of a business. It is the reason he cannot see there isn't one.**

Every single thing you would build next, someone with more data, more distribution, and more money either already ships or can ship in a fortnight -- and the four companies that got there before you are sitting on eleven ratings between them.
