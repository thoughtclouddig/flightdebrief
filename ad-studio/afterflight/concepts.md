# AfterFlight — Concept (LOCKED, user-authored)

Superseded the studio's original 3-pitch shortlist (kept below for the record). The
user wrote the concept directly — verbatim script retained as the canonical spine.

## Title: "Every Flight Builds"

**Core insight:** the emotional center isn't "CFIs don't debrief well" — it's what
happens to a student's brain after a demanding training flight. You land mentally
saturated, at the exact moment you're given the most valuable feedback of the
lesson. The flight pushes you. The debrief makes sense of it. AfterFlight makes
sure it stays with you.

**Tone:** cinematic, intimate, authentic. Starts intense and mentally crowded.
Becomes quiet and reflective after landing. Finishes with clarity, confidence, and
anticipation.

**Music:** minimal cinematic score. Tension/pulse during the flight. Opens up after
landing, warmer toward the end.

**Critical rule: the CFI is excellent, not rushed or negligent.** Thoughtful,
engaged, giving great feedback — that's what makes the insight land: even a great
debrief is fighting human memory. AfterFlight doesn't appear until roughly the
halfway mark (~0:33) — the first 30s sell pure recognition ("yes, that's exactly
what flight training feels like"), then the product arrives as the natural answer.
No talking-head testimonials, no conventional screen-recording product demo — the
UI is woven into the story, not a demo insert.

### Full timed script (source of truth for every segment below)

**0:00–0:08 — THE FLIGHT.** Tight, visceral cockpit shots. Student's hand on the
throttle. Eyes scanning instruments. Airspeed indicator. Runway moving beneath the
windshield. Instructor gesturing toward something outside. Radio chatter under the
music. Quick cuts, slightly handheld — we feel the workload, not just watch an
airplane fly. *VO: "A training flight can ask everything you've got."*

**0:08–0:16 — MENTAL SATURATION.** Turning base to final. A correction. Hands
working. Concentration. Touchdown. The airplane slows. Taxiing back. Then — engine
shuts down. Sudden quiet. Hold on the student's face. *VO: "You're learning.
Adjusting. Making mistakes. Fixing them. Trying to stay ahead of the airplane."*

**0:16–0:26 — THE DEBRIEF.** Student and instructor walk inside together. Water
bottle hits the table. Headset comes off. Sitting together, talking through the
flight. Instructor engaged, pointing something out; student nods. Reads as a good
debrief between two people who care about getting better. *VO: "Then you land, sit
down with your instructor, and talk through what just happened. What worked. What
didn't. What to work on next."*

**0:26–0:33 — THE PROBLEM.** Stay on the student listening. Instructor's voice
gradually softens beneath the music. Cut to later: student walking toward the
parking lot, flight bag over shoulder. A small pause before getting into the car.
*VO: "It's some of the most valuable feedback you'll get. And you're hearing it
when you're tired, overloaded... and already starting to forget."* Music drops.

**0:33–0:45 — AFTERFLIGHT.** Enters the story — not a giant product reveal. We see
the debrief captured naturally, then beautiful, fast shots of the actual interface:
what went well, needs work, next-lesson focus, student/CFI assessments, progress
over time, ACS connections. Everything feels like AfterFlight organizing the
conversation we just witnessed. *VO: "AfterFlight turns that conversation into
something you can keep. Your debrief. What went well. What needs work. And what
comes next."*

**0:45–0:54 — THE NEXT FLIGHT.** Different day, morning light. Student approaches
the airplane. Before climbing in, glances at their phone: "NEXT FLIGHT" — three
clear focus items from the previous lesson. Phone goes away. Preflight begins. Body
language is different this time — prepared, purposeful, ready. *VO: "So before your
next lesson, you don't have to remember where you left off. You already know what
you're there to work on."*

**0:54–1:00 — PAYOFF.** Student and instructor in the airplane. Engine starts.
Taxi. Beautiful shot as the airplane turns onto the runway. Cut just before takeoff
— or follow the aircraft climbing into morning light. AfterFlight logo. *VO: "Every
flight builds on the one before it."* **On screen:** "One debrief. Everything you
need for the next flight. AfterFlight."

### Render segmentation (script snapped to 4×15s Seedance jobs)
- **Segment A (0:00–0:15):** The Flight → Mental Saturation, through engine
  shutdown and the quiet held shot on the student's face.
- **Segment B (0:15–0:30):** The Debrief, into the start of The Problem (instructor
  vo starting to soften).
- **Segment C (0:30–0:45):** The Problem finish (parking lot) → AfterFlight reveal
  (UI woven into story, fast interface beats).
- **Segment D (0:45–0:60):** The Next Flight → Payoff → logo/on-screen line.

### Concept spine (non-negotiable in every frame)
1. The CFI reads as excellent and engaged, never rushed, distracted, or negligent —
   this is what makes the memory-saturation insight land.
2. AfterFlight's product/UI does not appear before ~0:33; the first half is pure
   recognition of the flight-training experience with no branding.
3. No talking-head testimonial framing, no conventional screen-recording demo
   insert — UI beats are woven into the story's own camera language (over-shoulder,
   hands on phone/tablet, wall screen glimpsed in the room), never a flat capture.
4. One consistent student + one consistent CFI throughout — same two people, same
   wardrobe, same aircraft, start to finish.
5. Warm-cool-warm light arc: workload tension (cockpit, natural daylight) → quiet
   interior debrief (warm lamp light) → morning golden hour for the next flight and
   payoff.

## Superseded shortlist (kept on record, not chosen)
1. **The Debrief Chain** — single relationship followed to a checkride pass; too
   thin on the flight-school angle to fill 60s alone.
2. **Ten Minutes Back** *(studio's original recommendation)* — multi-setting
   rushed-debrief angle; superseded by the user's stronger insight (the problem is
   memory/fatigue, not CFI negligence — and making the CFI look rushed undercuts
   the point).
3. **Every Flight Makes You Better** — closest in spirit to the locked concept, but
   single-buyer and without the "product enters at the midpoint" structure.
