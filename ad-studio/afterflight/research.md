# AfterFlight — Ad Research Brief

## True value
AfterFlight turns the 10 minutes after a training flight — normally lost to a rushed
parking-lot chat or nothing at all — into a structured, saved debrief: what happened,
what the instructor observed, what needs work, what the standard (ACS) requires, what
to study, what to practice next flight. The product's wedge is the debrief chain, not
a "score." It makes CFIs' spoken feedback permanent, trackable, and connected to real
checkride standards — for the student flying today and the flight school tracking
progress across its whole fleet of students.

## ICP resonance map
Two buyers, one workflow:
- **Student pilots** — anxious about checkride readiness, tired of vague "you did fine
  today" debriefs they can't act on later. They want to *see* progress: which ACS areas
  are solid, which need work, what to study before the next lesson. Confirms the real
  pain point found in flight-training discussion boards: students report barely getting
  a real debrief because the CFI is already late for the next lesson — the debrief gets
  skipped or rushed ([FlightAware forum](https://discussions.flightaware.com/t/new-student-with-a-question-about-cfis/10916)).
- **CFIs & flight-school admins** — want their verbal feedback to stick without extra
  paperwork, and want a defensible, structured training record across every student,
  not sticky notes and memory. Existing category (CloudAhoy, Instructeeze) proves the
  demand for structured post-flight review software already exists.

## Aesthetic field (sampled from product + brand docs)
- **Sampled from `score-phone-hangar.webp`**: overwhelmingly dark/near-black dominant
  tones (`#000000`, `#181818`, `#181800`) — the phone screen and dim indoor hangar
  lounge lighting — with warm neutral mid-tones (`#907860`, `#786048`) from skin, wood,
  and leather furniture. The one vivid accent, small in area but unmistakable, sits in
  the `#a84800`–`#c04800` range — the AfterFlight orange mark, dimmed by ambient light
  but clearly the only saturated color in frame.
- **Confirmed against brand docs** (`docs/marketing-design-brief.md`): true accent is
  `#f07621` brand orange — "the only saturated color," used sparingly as CTA/mark/accent
  against navy ink (`#101727`), gray body copy (`#68717D`), and white. Never a second
  accent color.
- **Photography style**: real aviation environments — ramps, hangars, cockpits, training
  rooms — always real photography, never illustration or generic SaaS iconography. Warm
  golden-hour or moody indoor practical light, not clinical studio light.
- **Motif**: the circular flight-score ring (seen on-screen in the product photo) is the
  product's signature visual device — a ring closing/filling is the one thing this brand
  owns visually.

## Off-brand blacklist
- No fake AI-score gauges or invented dashboards outside the real FlightScore ring.
- No implication that an algorithm grades the student or overrides the instructor — the
  CFI is always the authority; the product preserves and structures their judgment.
- Banned words (from brand copy voice): *unlock, supercharge, revolutionize, seamlessly,
  powered by AI, transform your training ecosystem, actionable insights, single source
  of truth.*
- No second saturated accent color competing with the brand orange.
- No stock-SaaS office people pointing at generic bar-chart dashboards.

## Category cliché blacklist (avoid all five)
1. Generic "team high-fiving around a laptop" SaaS stock-photo energy.
2. Cockpit hero shot with dramatic sunset flare and no product ever shown (all mood,
   no substance — common in aviation-adjacent ads).
3. A disembodied hand tapping a glowing dashboard full of meaningless charts.
4. Slow-motion student pilot walking toward a plane in silhouette (aviation-training
   ad's version of the perfume-ad walk).
5. A CFI "grading" a paper checklist with a red pen — visually implies grading/judgment
   rather than the product's real conversational-debrief value prop.
